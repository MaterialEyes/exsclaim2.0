      The development of efficient and low-cost photocathodes capable of directly utilizing seawater as an electrolyte is essential for sustainable hydrogen production. Here, we report the fabrication of a CuO1−X–poly(N-methylpyrrole) (PNMP) nanocomposite exhibiting a crystallite size of approximately 10 nm and an optical bandgap of 1.92 eV, enabling broad-spectrum light harvesting. The nanocomposite was deposited onto a poly(1H-pyrrole) (P1HP) interlayer to construct a CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode, followed by the incorporation of Ti3C2 MXene to produce a Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP heterostructured photocathode. The combination of oxygen-deficient CuO1−X domains, conductive polymer networks, and the metallic Ti3C2 framework promotes photon capture, accelerates charge-carrier transport, and suppresses interfacial recombination. Consequently, the photocurrent density increased from −0.05 mA cm−2 for CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP to −0.13 mA cm−2 following Ti3C2 integration. The Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode achieved a hydrogen evolution rate corresponding to approximately 6 µmol h−1 cm−2 in an equivalent seawater electrolyte. The enhanced performance originates from the synergistic interaction between the MXene conductive pathways and the defect-engineered CuO1−X semiconductor matrix, which collectively facilitate efficient photoinduced charge separation and transport. These findings demonstrate a promising strategy for the development of scalable and economically viable photocathodes for direct solar-driven hydrogen generation from seawater.

      1 Introduction

      Growing concerns regarding the long-term availability of fossil-based energy resources, coupled with the environmental consequences of their extensive use, have stimulated intensive research into alternative energy pathways. Renewable technologies are increasingly viewed as essential components of a sustainable energy future. Among these technologies, solar energy has attracted particular attention because it is widely accessible, continuously replenished by nature, and capable of delivering energy with a substantially reduced environmental footprint. In this context, photocatalysis has emerged as an effective strategy for directly harvesting solar energy and converting it into chemical fuels, particularly hydrogen (H2), which is widely regarded as a clean, high-energy-density energy carrier for future energy systems.1 

      Photocatalytic water splitting enables the conversion of water (H2O) into hydrogen and oxygen through photoexcited charge carriers generated in semiconductor materials under solar irradiation. Beyond conventional freshwater systems, recent advances have utilized naturally abundant and low-cost feedstocks such as seawater and wastewater. This approach not only mitigates freshwater consumption but also integrates energy conversion with environmental remediation, thereby aligning with the principles of sustainable development and green chemistry. However, efficient hydrogen production from complex aqueous matrices remains challenging due to the presence of competing ions and potential catalytic deactivation.2–4 

      Inorganic semiconductors, including oxides and sulfides that extensively explored as photocatalysts owing to their structural robustness, chemical stability, and tunable electronic configurations. Their functionality is primarily derived from the generation of electron–hole pairs upon light absorption, which drive the redox reactions necessary for hydrogen and oxygen evolution. Despite these advantages, their practical application is hindered by intrinsic limitations such as wide band gaps, restricted photon absorption, and rapid charge-carrier recombination, all of which contribute to low photocurrent density and limited hydrogen evolution efficiency.5,6 

      To overcome these drawbacks, significant attention has been directed toward emerging functional materials of carbon nanostructures or conductor polymer. These materials offer several advantages, including low-cost synthesis, structural flexibility, environmental compatibility, and facile molecular-level tunability. Nevertheless, their long-term stability under photocatalytic operating conditions remains a critical issue, particularly due to photocorrosion, structural degradation, and inefficient charge transport, which collectively limit their practical deployment in hydrogen production systems.7 

      More recently, two-dimensional (2D) materials, particularly MXenes, have attracted considerable interest as advanced components in photocatalytic architectures. Owing to their unique layered transition metal carbide/nitride structures, MXenes that has strong interfacial interaction capabilities with electrical conductivity. These properties enable rapid charge transfer, efficient separation of photoinduced electron–hole pairs, and enhanced catalytic activity at the solid–liquid interface. In addition, their tunable surface chemistry and excellent hydrophilicity further promote charge-carrier mobility and improve overall photocatalytic performance.8,9 

      Despite these advances, many reported photocatalytic systems still rely on highly corrosive electrolytes such as H2SO4 or NaOH, or require sacrificial agents including NaBH4 and NH4OH to enhance reaction kinetics. Such conditions not only increase operational complexity and environmental risk but also obscure the intrinsic catalytic behavior of the developed materials, limiting their relevance for practical applications. Therefore, the development of robust photocatalytic systems capable of operating under environmentally benign and realistic aqueous conditions remains a critical scientific challenge.10 

      This study reports the fabrication of a CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode, subsequently engineered through the integration of Ti3C2 MXene to construct a Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP heterostructured system. The synergistic coupling of oxygen-deficient CuO1−X domains, conductive polymeric networks, and the metallically conductive Ti3C2 framework collectively enhances photon harvesting, facilitates rapid charge-carrier migration, and effectively suppresses interfacial electron–hole recombination. This hierarchical architecture establishes efficient charge-transfer pathways across the interface, thereby improving the overall photoelectrochemical response. The as-fabricated photocathode is systematically evaluated under monochromatic illumination to elucidate its wavelength-dependent photoresponse, where the current density in light (Jph) serves as a key quantitative metric for performance assessment. The device demonstrates a rational balance between structural simplicity and functional efficiency, benefiting from a low-cost and facile fabrication strategy that is compatible with scalable processing. These attributes, combined with its enhanced photoelectrochemical performance in seawater environments, highlight its potential for practical hydrogen evolution applications and future commercial deployment in solar-driven water-splitting technologies.

      1.1 Materials and characterization

      Copper sulfate (CuSO4, 99.9%), titanium aluminum carbide (Ti3AlC2, 99.9%), and N-methylpyrrole (99.9%) were utilized as precursor materials and obtained from commercial suppliers. Magnesium chloride (MgCl2, 99.8%), potassium bicarbonate (KHCO3, 99.9%), sodium chloride (NaCl, 99.9%), sodium sulfate (Na2SO4, 99.8%), and ammonium persulfate ((NH4)2S2O8, ≥99%) were used as received without additional purification. All aqueous solutions and experimental preparations were conducted using deionized water.

      The molecular bonding configuration and functional groups of these materials were examined through FTIR (FTIR, Bruker). The crystallographic phases and structural ordering were investigated using XRD (XRD, PANalytical X'Pert PRO) operated with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å). The electronic oxidation states were analyzed by XPS (XPS, Kratos Analytical). The morphological characteristics and nanoscale architectures were evaluated using SEM (SEM, ZEISS) and TEM (TEM, JEOL). Optical absorption behavior and light-harvesting properties were assessed using a UV-visible spectrophotometer (PerkinElmer).

      1.2 Preparation procedures

      1.2.1 Synthesis of Ti3C2 MXene

      Ti3C2 MXene was synthesized through the selective exfoliation and etching of the Al atomic layers from the Ti3AlC2 MAX phase. In a typical procedure, lithium fluoride (LiF, 1 g) was gradually dissolved in 20 mL HCl under continuous magnetic stirring to generate the in situ etching medium. Subsequently, Ti3AlC2 powder was slowly introduced into the acidic solution, followed by continuous stirring for 4 days to ensure effective removal of the Al layers and complete exfoliation of the layered structure. After completion of the etching process, the obtained suspension was repeatedly washed using isopropanol to remove residual reaction byproducts and unreacted species. The resulting material was obtained as a suspended of Ti3C2 MXene 2D materials.

      1.2.2 The Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode fabrication

      The Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode was fabricated through a sequential multistep deposition strategy involving the formation of a P1HP interfacial layer, followed by the growth of the CuO1−X–PNMP active layer, and finally the incorporation of the Ti3C2 MXene overlayer.

      Initially, the P1HP thin film was synthesized directly onto a glass substrate through the oxidative polymerization of pyrrole in acidic medium. (NH4)2S2O8 was employed as the oxidizing agent to initiate polymer formation. The concentrations of pyrrole monomer, oxidant, and acid medium were adjusted to 0.06, 0.12, and 1.0 M, respectively. The polymerization process resulted in the uniform deposition of the P1HP conductive layer on the substrate surface.

      Subsequently, the CuO1−X–PNMP layer was deposited through an in situ oxidative polymerization process involving N-methylpyrrole in the presence of CuSO4 precursor under acidic conditions. Ammonium persulfate was utilized as the oxidizing agent to promote simultaneous polymer growth and copper oxide formation. The concentrations of N-methylpyrrole monomer, ammonium persulfate, CuSO4, and acid medium were maintained at 0.06, 0.07, 0.07, and 1.0 M, respectively. This process enabled the formation of an oxygen-deficient CuO1−X hybrid structure embedded within the conductive PNMP matrix (Fig. S1(a)).

      Finally, the Ti3C2 MXene layer was introduced onto the CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP structure through a physical casting approach. Approximately 0.5 mL of the Ti3C2 dispersion was uniformly deposited over the surface of the prepared film, followed by thermal treatment at 60 °C for 3 h to enhance film adhesion and structural integration. This sequential assembly resulted in the formation of the final Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode architecture.

      1.3 The Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode photoelectrochemical testing

      The photoelectrochemical performance of the Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode was investigated through seawater splitting reactions using simulated with the same chemical composition seawater electrolyte. The fabricated photocathode is the working electrode for hydrogen evolution under light irradiation.

      The simulated seawater electrolyte was prepared by dissolving inorganic salts with concentrations corresponding to the major ionic constituents of natural seawater. The electrolyte composition included KHCO3 (0.24 g L−1), NaCl (38.38 g L−1), CaCl2 (2.43 g L−1), MgCl2 (19.08 g L−1), and Na2SO4 (5.26 g L−1). Trace heavy-metal species naturally present in seawater were intentionally excluded to evaluate their influence on the photoelectrochemical hydrogen generation process.

      Photoelectrochemical measurements were conducted using a CHI608E electrochemical workstation under illumination conditions. The hydrogen evolution activity of the photocathode was evaluated through linear sweep voltammetry by monitoring the current density in light (Jph) relative to the dark current density (J0). The enhanced photocurrent response under illumination was associated with the photoinduced reduction reactions responsible for hydrogen generation from seawater, (Fig. S1(b)).

      The spectral photoresponse of the photocathode was investigated under different photon energies of 3.6, 2.8, 2.3, and 1.7 eV. These monochromatic photon energies were obtained using optical filtering systems that selectively converted white-light irradiation into controlled wavelength regions prior to illumination of the photocathode surface. The corresponding photocurrent densities generated under each photon energy were subsequently recorded.       2 Results and discussion

      2.1 Physicochemical characterization

      2.1.1 Structural, crystallographic, and optical properties

      The FTIR spectra of PNMP, CuO1−X–PNMP, and Ti3C2 MXene reveal clear structural differences following composite formation. Pristine PNMP exhibits characteristic absorption bands at 591, 870, 1052, 1189, 1398, and 1629 cm−1, which are attributed to the vibrational modes of the polymer backbone. Most of these features are retained in the CuO1−X–PNMP nanocomposite, although slight shifts in peak position are observed, indicating electronic interactions between the copper oxide phase and the polymer chains.

      The CuO1−X–PNMP spectrum displays additional bands at 615, 753, 1040, 1280, 1397, 1496, and 1578 cm−1, reflecting modifications in the local bonding environment following incorporation of oxygen-deficient copper oxide. The broad band centred at 3123 cm−1, assigned to N–H stretching vibrations, exhibits a slight shift relative to that of pristine PNMP, further supporting interfacial coupling between the inorganic and organic constituents.11  The emergence of the low-wavenumber bands at 615 and 753 cm−1 is associated with Cu–O-related vibrational modes and their interaction with the polymer framework. The overall spectral shifts indicate charge redistribution and structural reorganization within the polymer matrix induced by CuO1−X incorporation, confirming successful nanocomposite formation.

      The Ti3C2 MXene spectrum is characterized by bands located at 1095, 1400, and 1629 cm−1, which are associated with carbon-containing surface functionalities and C–C/C═C vibrations within the MXene structure. Additional absorption features at 608 and 714 cm−1 are attributed to Ti–O vibrations arising from surface-terminated titanium species. The coexistence of these characteristic bands confirms the preservation of the Ti3C2 MXene framework and its surface functionalities, which are expected to facilitate interfacial charge transport and enhance the electronic coupling within the hybrid architecture.

      The crystalline characteristics of the CuO1−X–PNMP nanocomposite and Ti3C2 MXene were examined by XRD, as shown in Fig. 1b. The pristine PNMP exhibited multiple diffraction peaks within the 2θ range of 17.4–36.6°, indicating the presence of ordered domains within the polymer matrix. These reflections suggest that the synthesized PNMP possesses a partially crystalline nature, arising from the regular arrangement of polymer chains.

      Following the incorporation of CuO1−X, noticeable changes in the diffraction pattern were observed, confirming the successful formation of the CuO1−X–PNMP nanocomposite. The characteristic PNMP reflections remained visible within the 13.8–35.4° region, indicating that the polymeric framework was preserved after composite formation. Moreover, six additional diffraction peaks appeared at 32.3°, 36.8°, 40.1°, 47.5°, 50.7°, and 57.2°, which were indexed to the (110), (002), (111), (−202), (020), and (202) crystallographic planes of monoclinic CuO1−X, respectively (JCPDS No. 48-1548).12  The emergence of these well-defined reflections confirms the successful growth of crystalline CuO1−X within the PNMP matrix. Furthermore, the increased number and intensity of diffraction peaks compared with pristine PNMP indicate an enhancement in the overall crystallinity of the composite, which is expected to facilitate charge transport and improve the electronic properties of the material.

      The XRD pattern of Ti3C2 MXene displayed distinct diffraction peaks centered at 20.8°, 33.9°, 41.4°, 44.9°, and 56.2°, corresponding to the (012), (006), (014), (013), and (017) planes, respectively. These reflections are characteristic of the layered Ti3C2 structure and confirm the successful synthesis of the MXene phase. In addition to the MXene reflections, several secondary diffraction peaks were observed, indicating partial oxidation of the Ti3C2 surface during the etching and exfoliation processes. Peaks located at 26.9°, 36.4°, and 37.5° can be assigned to the rutile TiO2 phase, whereas reflections at 37.5° and 48.2° correspond to anatase TiO2 (JCPDS No. 75-1537).13,14  The coexistence of rutile and anatase phases suggests the formation of a Ti3C2/TiO2 heterostructure on the MXene surface. Such a hybrid architecture is advantageous for photoelectrochemical applications, as the interfacial interaction between Ti3C2 and TiO2 can promote charge separation, accelerate electron transport, and suppress charge recombination, thereby enhancing the overall photocatalytic and photoelectrochemical performance of the resulting photocathode.

      Application of the Debye–Scherrer formula (eqn (2))15,16  to the most intense diffraction reflection enabled the determination of the crystallite size of the CuO1−X–PNMP nanocomposite. Analysis of the corresponding FWHM value indicated an average crystallite dimension close to 10 nm, confirming the formation of nanocrystalline CuO1−X domains uniformly embedded within the polymeric matrix.

      The interplanar spacing (d) was determined using Bragg's relation (eqn (3)),17  yielding a value of 2.5 Å, which is consistent with the characteristic lattice planes of CuO-based nanostructures. In addition, the lattice strain (ε), calculated from peak broadening analysis (eqn (4)),17  was found to be 7.0 × 10−2, indicating a significant degree of microstrain within the composite structure.

      Collectively, these structural parameters confirm the successful formation of a well-defined nanocrystalline framework and suggest the presence of strong interfacial interactions and lattice distortions at the CuO1−X-polymer boundary, which are expected to play a crucial role in modulating charge transport and interfacial electronic properties.

      The nanocrystalline nature of the CuO1−X–PNMP composite is expected to play a critical role in determining its photoelectrochemical performance. The reduced crystallite dimensions substantially increase the interfacial contact area between the oxygen-deficient copper oxide domains and the conductive polymer matrix, generating a high density of active sites for charge-transfer reactions. Such an architecture promotes strong electronic coupling at the inorganic–organic interface, facilitating rapid transport of photogenerated charge carriers while suppressing bulk and interfacial recombination processes.

      Beyond the structural advantages associated with nanoscale crystallinity, oxygen vacancy defects in CuO1−X act as electronically active centers that reshape the semiconductor's electronic environment, influencing local charge polarization and modifying its overall electronic structure. These vacancies can act as electron-rich centres, enhancing visible-light absorption and promoting the formation of localized electric fields at the semiconductor surface.18–20  The presence of an intrinsic electric field accelerates the dissociation of photoinduced charge carriers and minimizes their recombination probability. As a result, electrons are efficiently transported to the electrolyte-contact region, where they contribute to reduction reactions with greater effectiveness. The synergistic integration of oxygen-deficient CuO1−X with the conjugated PNMP framework further enhances charge extraction and transport. The conductive polymer serves as an efficient pathway for carrier migration, while the CuO1−X nanodomains provide abundant catalytic sites for interfacial redox reactions. Under illumination, this cooperative charge-transfer mechanism accelerates electron delivery to the seawater/electrode interface, where reduction reactions occur, ultimately promoting hydrogen evolution. Collectively, the combination of nanocrystalline morphology, enlarged interfacial area, vacancy-engineered electronic structure, and efficient charge-transport pathways is expected to improve photon harvesting, charge-separation efficiency, and interfacial reaction kinetics. These attributes provide a mechanistic basis for the enhanced photoelectrochemical activity of the CuO1−X–PNMP composite during seawater reduction and sustainable hydrogen production.

      The optical characteristics of the CuO1−X–PNMP nanocomposite, pristine PNMP, and Ti3C2 MXene were investigated using UV-visible absorption spectroscopy, as presented in Fig. 1c. The CuO1−X–PNMP nanocomposite exhibited a broad and intense absorption profile extending beyond 600 nm, demonstrating its strong capability to harvest photons across a substantial portion of the visible spectrum. In contrast, pristine PNMP displayed an absorption edge near 570 nm with comparatively lower absorption intensity, indicating a more limited light-harvesting capacity. The Ti3C2 MXene showed pronounced absorption within the ultraviolet and short-wavelength visible regions, consistent with its electronic structure and surface plasmon-related optical response.

      The enhanced optical absorption observed for the CuO1−X–PNMP nanocomposite reflects the effective electronic interaction between the CuO1−X nanocrystals and the conductive polymer matrix. Such coupling promotes the formation of additional electronic states and facilitates broader photon utilization, thereby improving the overall light-harvesting capability of the composite.

      To further elucidate the electronic structure of the investigated materials, the bandgap energies (Eg) were determined using Tauc analysis (eqn (5))11  through the direct transition: n = 1/2. The bandgap values were extracted from the linear regions of the corresponding Tauc plots by extrapolating the absorption coefficient (α) as a function of photon energy. The CuO1−X–PNMP nanocomposite exhibited a bandgap energy of approximately 1.92 eV, which is lower than that of pristine PNMP (2.10 eV) and Ti3C2 MXene (2.50 eV). The reduction in bandgap energy following composite formation indicates the creation of a more favorable electronic structure for visible-light absorption and photoinduced charge generation.

      The incorporation of Ti3C2 MXene is expected to significantly enhance the photoelectrochemical performance of the composite owing to its exceptional electrical conductivity, metallic character, and strong light–matter interactions. Upon illumination, Ti3C2 MXene can facilitate the generation and transport of energetic charge carriers,21,22  while its highly conductive framework promotes rapid electron extraction and migration across the electrode architecture. The resulting charge redistribution contributes to the formation of localized electric fields that accelerate carrier separation and suppress electron–hole recombination. Simultaneously, the oxygen-deficient CuO1−X phase introduces a high concentration of vacancy-induced electronic states that modify the electronic structure and increase the density of charge-transfer-active sites. These oxygen vacancies enhance visible-light harvesting and promote the accumulation of photogenerated electrons at the semiconductor surface. The synergistic interaction between Ti3C2 MXene and CuO1−X establishes an efficient charge-transfer network, enabling rapid electron migration from the photoactive domains toward the conductive MXene sheets. The intimate interfacial coupling among Ti3C2 MXene, CuO1−X, and PNMP is therefore expected to facilitate directional charge transport, enhance carrier lifetime, and increase the flux of reduction-active electrons reaching the electrolyte interface. Such cooperative effects improve the kinetics of seawater reduction and hydrogen evolution reactions, resulting in enhanced photoelectrochemical hydrogen production. The combination of MXene-assisted charge extraction, vacancy-engineered CuO1−X electronic states, and the conductive PNMP framework estimates a highly platform for solar-driven hydrogen generation.

      The UV-vis-NIR absorption spectrum of the Ti3C2 MXene (Fig. S2) exhibits a broad optical response extending throughout the visible and near-infrared (NIR) regions, indicating its metallic-like electronic behavior. The absorption intensity remains relatively high over a wide wavelength range, with a weak broad absorption feature observed around 900–930 nm, followed by a decrease at longer wavelengths. Such broadband absorption is characteristic of Ti3C2 MXene and has been widely attributed to the collective oscillation of free charge carriers associated with its high electrical conductivity and metallic nature. Rather than exhibiting a sharp localized surface plasmon resonance peak similar to noble metal nanoparticles, Ti3C2 MXene generally displays a broad plasmonic response extending from the visible to the NIR region owing to its two-dimensional layered structure and high free-electron density. This broad optical response enhances light harvesting and promotes the generation of energetic charge carriers, thereby facilitating interfacial charge transfer and contributing to the improved photoelectrochemical hydrogen evolution performance of the Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode.

      The high-resolution Cu spectrum (Fig. 2b) exhibits a prominent Cu 2p3/2 peak centered at approximately 935.0 eV, which is characteristic of Cu2+ species through an oxygen deficiency oxygen and confirms the formation of the CuO1−X phase within the composite.23  This assignment is in agreement with the XRD analysis, which verified the presence of crystalline monoclinic CuO1−X. The O 1s spectrum (Fig. 2c) displays a dominant peak at 531.7 eV, corresponding to oxygen species associated with the CuO1−X lattice and surface oxygen-containing groups. The combined Cu and O spectral features provide strong evidence for the successful formation of the CuO1−X–PNMP nanocomposite.

      The XPS survey spectrum confirms the presence of C, N, O, and Cu. With Cu of 1.0%, and after excluding adventitious carbon and considering the deconvoluted Cu 2p and O 1s spectra, the copper oxide phase is consistent with an oxygen-deficient composition, approximately CuO0.95, confirming the formation of CuO1−X.

      The XPS survey spectrum of Ti3C2 MXene (Fig. 2d) confirms the presence of Ti, C, and O elements, consistent with the expected composition of the synthesized MXene. The O 1s transition appears at approximately 531.5 eV, indicating oxygen-containing surface terminations and partially oxidized titanium species. The high-resolution Ti 2p spectrum (Fig. 2e) contains two principal contributions located at 455.6 and 459.1 eV. The peak at 455.6 eV is assigned to Ti species within the Ti3C2 framework, whereas the component at 459.1 eV is attributed to TiO2 formed through partial oxidation of the MXene surface. These results indicate the coexistence of Ti3C2 and surface titanium oxide species.24 

      Further insight into the carbon environment is provided by the C 1s spectrum (Fig. 2f), which exhibits characteristic peaks at 284.6 and 286.3 eV. These contributions correspond to C–C/C═C and C–O bonding configurations, respectively. The presence of these carbon species, together with the Ti- and O-related transitions, highlights the chemically functionalized nature of the MXene surface. Such surface functionalities are expected to promote interfacial charge transfer and strengthen the electronic coupling between Ti3C2 MXene and the CuO1−X–PNMP matrix, thereby enhancing charge separation and photoelectrochemical activity in the developed photocathode.

      2.1.2 Morphological architecture and surface features

      The morphological evolution of the CuO1−X–PNMP nanocomposite was examined by SEM analysis (Fig. 3a and b) in comparison with pristine PNMP (Fig. 3c), revealing a pronounced restructuring of the polymeric architecture upon incorporation of the inorganic phase. Pristine PNMP exhibits relatively compact and weakly textured particles with an average size of ∼120 nm. In contrast, the CuO1−X–PNMP composite displays a refined hierarchical morphology composed of secondary particles with reduced effective dimensions (∼100 nm), which are themselves constructed from primary nanoscale building units (∼15 nm). This multilevel organization indicates that the introduction of oxygen-deficient CuO1−X disrupts the original packing of the polymer chains and induces heterogeneous nucleation within the matrix, thereby promoting the formation of densely interconnected nanodomains.

The high aspect ratio and metallic conductivity of Ti3C2 enable efficient in-plane and interlayer electron transport, while its extended surface area provides abundant anchoring sites for hybrid integration with CuO1−X–PNMP domains.

      The synergistic integration of hierarchical CuO1−X–PNMP nanostructures with the two-dimensional Ti3C2 MXene network establishes a multiscale charge-transport architecture. In this configuration, CuO1−X acts as the primary photoactive and defect-engineered semiconductor, PNMP functions as a conductive polymeric bridge facilitating interfacial charge migration, and MXene serves as a rapid electron-transport highway. This cooperative framework enhances charge separation efficiency, prolongs carrier lifetime, and maximizes electron flux toward the electrolyte interface. Collectively, these structural advantages are expected to significantly improve light harvesting and accelerate the kinetics of seawater hydrogen evolution reactions.

      2.2 Green hydrogen generation using the ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode

      Photoelectrochemical hydrogen production was investigated using the fabricated Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode (working electrode) configuration employing seawater as the electrolyte. Under illumination, the engineered heterostructure efficiently drives the H2 evolution reaction through the reduction of water molecules at the photocathode surface, resulting in sustainable H2 generation without the addition of external sacrificial reagents.

      The remarkable photoelectrochemical activity of the Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP architecture originates from the synergistic integration of its individual components. CuO1−X, PNMP, and P1HP constitute p-type photoactive domains that exhibit broad visible-light absorption, while the partial surface oxidation of Ti3C2 MXene generates TiO2-rich regions with n-type semiconducting characteristics. The intimate interfacial contact between these p-type and n-type constituents promotes the formation of built-in electric fields, facilitating efficient charge separation and suppressing electron–hole recombination. Simultaneously, the metallic conductivity of Ti3C2 MXene provides rapid electron transport pathways, enabling the photogenerated electrons to migrate efficiently toward the electrolyte interface. Furthermore, oxygen-vacancy defects within CuO1−X introduce localized electronic states that enhance visible-light harvesting and charge-carrier density. The combined effects of defect engineering, heterojunction formation, and MXene-assisted charge transport substantially improve photon utilization and photocurrent generation, ultimately accelerating the hydrogen evolution process.

      Seawater serves as a particularly attractive electrolyte owing to its intrinsic ionic composition. The presence of these occurring ions enhances electrolyte conductivity and promotes interfacial charge transfer operation. Unlike conventional hydrogen-production systems that frequently require strongly acidic or alkaline electrolytes, such as H2SO4 or NaOH, the present approach utilizes seawater directly,25  eliminating the need for corrosive chemical additives. This strategy not only reduces operational complexity and material degradation but also improves the environmental and economic viability of large-scale hydrogen production. Consequently, seawater functions as a promising conductive medium capable of sustaining efficient photoelectrochemical reactions while avoiding the drawbacks associated with harsh electrolyte environments.

      P1HP serves as an interfacial conductive layer that plays both structural and electronic roles in the photocathode architecture. During the fabrication process, the P1HP layer provides a uniform and strongly adhered conductive surface, promoting the homogeneous nucleation and growth of the CuO1−X–PNMP active layer and facilitating the subsequent deposition of the Ti3C2 MXene. This interfacial layer enhances the structural stability of the multilayer electrode while improving the electrical contact between the individual components. Furthermore, owing to its conjugated conducting polymer framework, P1HP functions as an effective hole-transport layer by facilitating hole extraction from the photoactive CuO1−X–PNMP layer and suppressing interfacial electron–hole recombination. Consequently, the incorporation of P1HP promotes more efficient charge separation and transport, leading to the enhanced photoelectrochemical hydrogen evolution performance observed for the Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode.

      As shown in Fig. 4a, the Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode exhibits a pronounced photoelectrochemical response in seawater electrolyte, achieving a photocurrent density (Jph) of approximately −0.13 mA cm−2, whereas the corresponding J0 remains limited to nearly −0.01 mA cm−2. In contrast, the CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode without Ti3C2 MXene delivers a substantially lower photocurrent density of approximately −0.05 mA cm−2 while maintaining a similar dark current density. The nearly threefold enhancement in photocurrent following Ti3C2 incorporation highlights the critical role of the MXene-based architecture in promoting photoinduced charge generation and utilization during the seawater reduction process. The remarkable improvement can be for the synergistic interaction between the highly conductive Ti3C2 MXene sheets, the oxygen-deficient CuO1−X semiconductor, and the PNMP/P1HP polymeric framework. The metallic conductivity of Ti3C2 facilitates rapid electron extraction and transport, suppressing charge recombination within the heterostructure.26,27  Simultaneously, the surface titanium oxide species naturally formed on the MXene layers contribute additional photoactive sites and interfacial energy levels that enhance light harvesting across a broad spectral region. The presence of oxygen vacancies within CuO1−X further promotes charge-carrier generation by introducing localized electronic states, thereby increasing visible-light absorption and improving charge-transfer kinetics. Under illumination, the efficient separation of photogenerated electron–hole pairs enables the migration of electrons toward the photocathode/electrolyte interface, where they participate in proton reduction and hydrogen evolution reactions. The significantly lower dark current density confirms that the measured cathodic response predominantly originates from photoinduced processes rather than parasitic electrochemical reactions. Furthermore, the complex ionic environment of seawater may contribute to enhanced interfacial conductivity and accelerated reaction kinetics, facilitating more efficient charge transfer across the electrode–electrolyte interface.

      The photoelectrochemical performance of the Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode is further validated by under light ON/OFF illumination cycles, as presented in Fig. 4b. The Ti3C2–TiO2 interface provides an efficient electron-transport pathway while simultaneously enhancing resistance against surface degradation. Moreover, the conductive polymer network serves as a protective framework that suppresses photocorrosion and preserves interfacial charge-transfer efficiency during prolonged operation.

      The practical applicability of the Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode was further evaluated in natural seawater (Red seawater, Hurghada city, Egypt), and the corresponding photoelectrochemical performance is presented in Fig. S3. The photocathode exhibited a pronounced photoresponse, with the photocurrent density increasing from a dark current (J0) of approximately −0.01 mA cm−2 to Jph of approximately −0.08 mA cm−2 under light illumination. This nearly eightfold enhancement demonstrates the excellent photoresponsivity of the fabricated photocathode in natural seawater. Moreover, the results indicate that the naturally occurring ionic species in seawater effectively participate in the interfacial electrochemical processes, enabling efficient photoelectrochemical hydrogen generation without the need for additional sacrificial agents or supporting electrolytes.

      The consistent photocurrent generation observed throughout the ON/OFF cycles confirms that the photocathode maintains its photoactivity without significant deterioration, highlighting its excellent durability and resistance to the harsh ionic environment of seawater. Such characteristics are particularly important for practical photoelectrochemical hydrogen production systems, where long-term operational stability is essential for sustainable performance. Consequently, the Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP heterostructure combines high photoresponsivity with robust durability, making it a promising candidate for scalable and long-lasting solar-driven hydrogen generation applications.

      The spectral photoresponse of the Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode was investigated to determine the optical window over which the device can effectively drive the reduction reaction, as illustrated in Fig. 5a. The photocathode was examined under monochromatic illumination at 340, 440, 540, and 730 nm, corresponding to photon energies of approximately 3.6, 2.8, 2.3, and 1.7 eV, respectively (Fig. 5b). The resulting Jph demonstrate that the device remains highly photoactive across a remarkably broad spectral range extending from the ultraviolet to the near-infrared region, highlighting its ability to utilize a substantial portion of the solar spectrum for photoelectrochemical hydrogen production. The strong photocurrent response observed under 340, 440, and 540 nm illumination is attributed to the efficient absorption of photons possessing energies greater than the optical bandgap of the CuO1−X–PNMP component (∼1.92 eV). When light is absorbed by the semiconductor, electrons gain sufficient energy to transition from the valence band to the conduction band, resulting in the formation of electron–hole pairs. Generating charge carriers that are effectively separated through the heterojunction interfaces. The oxygen-deficient nature of CuO1−X plays a critical role in this process, as oxygen vacancies introduce localized electronic states that enhance visible-light absorption and facilitate charge transport by reducing carrier recombination.29  Simultaneously, the conjugated PNMP matrix provides an extended π-electron network that promotes charge delocalization and facilitates carrier migration toward the electrode/electrolyte interface.

The incorporation of two-dimensional Ti3C2 nanosheets increases light scattering and prolongs the optical path length within the photoactive layer, thereby enhancing photon absorption. Simultaneously, the rough and porous architecture provides a high density of accessible reaction sites and enlarges the interfacial area exposed to the electrolyte. These structural features collectively improve both light harvesting and charge-transfer kinetics, resulting in enhanced photoelectrochemical performance.

      The photocurrent density was evaluated over a wide potential range to investigate the potential-dependent photoelectrochemical behavior of the fabricated photocathodes. As shown in the LSV curves, the photocurrent density gradually increased with increasing cathodic bias and reached its maximum value at −0.8 V vs. RHE, indicating the most favorable balance between photogenerated charge separation, electron transport, and interfacial hydrogen evolution kinetics. Accordingly, −0.8 V vs. RHE was selected as the operating potential for comparing the photoelectrochemical performance and hydrogen production efficiency of the investigated electrodes. The measured photocurrent densities, ranging from approximately −0.075 to −0.11 mA cm−2 across the investigated wavelengths, confirm the exceptional broadband photoactivity of the photocathode. The relatively small variation in photocurrent with increasing wavelength further indicates efficient utilization of lower-energy photons, which is highly advantageous for practical solar-energy conversion systems because a significant fraction of solar irradiation lies within the visible and near-infrared regions. Consequently, the Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP heterostructure demonstrates a synergistic combination of plasmon-enhanced photon harvesting, efficient charge separation, rapid electron transport, and robust interfacial reaction kinetics, establishing it as a highly promising photocathode for solar-driven hydrogen generation from seawater over an exceptionally wide spectral range.

      2.3 The mechanism and state of the art

      The exceptional hydrogen-generation performance of the Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode originates from the synergistic integration of multiple photoactive components possessing complementary optical and electronic properties. Upon illumination, the heterostructured architecture efficiently harvests incident photons across a broad spectral range, leading to the generation of electron–hole pairs within the semiconductor constituents. The partially oxidized Ti3C2 MXene surface, containing photoactive TiO2 domains, acts as an electron-accepting and transport layer owing to its favorable electronic structure and high carrier mobility. Simultaneously, the CuO1−X semiconductor and the conductive PNMP/P1HP polymer framework contribute additional photogenerated charge carriers, thereby increasing the overall electron population available for reduction reactions.

      The intimate interfacial contact between TiO2, Ti3C2 MXene, CuO1−X, and the polymeric layers establishes a cascade charge-transfer pathway that promotes efficient carrier separation while suppressing electron–hole recombination. Electrons generated within the CuO1−X and polymeric domains are rapidly transferred toward the TiO2/Ti3C2 interface, where they accumulate owing to the favorable band alignment and the excellent conductivity of the MXene network. Furthermore, the plasmonic characteristics associated with the oxygen-deficient CuO1−X phase and the Ti3C2 structure enhance local electromagnetic fields and increase photon utilization efficiency, producing energetic charge carriers with improved reduction capability. This plasmon-assisted process contributes to the generation of highly reactive electrons that can effectively participate in interfacial redox reactions.30  At the photocathode–electrolyte interface, the accumulated electrons are injected into the surrounding seawater, where they drive proton and water reduction reactions, ultimately leading to molecular hydrogen evolution. Meanwhile, the photogenerated holes migrate in the opposite direction through the heterostructure, minimizing charge recombination and sustaining continuous electron flow toward the reaction sites. The rapid extraction of electrons and the efficient transport of holes collectively establish a stable charge-separation process, which is essential for maintaining high photoelectrochemical activity. The hierarchical architecture, broad optical absorption, plasmon-enhanced charge generation, and efficient interfacial carrier transport collectively account for the remarkable hydrogen-production capability of the fabricated photocathode. As a result, the Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP heterostructure achieves a hydrogen evolution rate of ∼6.0 µmol h−1 cm−2, demonstrating its effectiveness as a robust photoelectrode for direct solar-driven hydrogen production from seawater. The obtained performance highlights the importance of combining conductive MXene platforms, oxygen-deficient semiconductors, and conjugated polymer networks to maximize photon utilization and charge-conversion efficiency in next-generation photoelectrochemical energy systems.

      The excellent charge-transfer capability of the CuO1−X–PNMP nanocomposite was further evaluated by EIS using a glassy carbon electrode in seawater electrolyte. As shown in Fig. S4, the nanocomposite exhibits a low charge-transfer resistance (RCT) of approximately 1.7 Ω, indicating rapid interfacial electron-transfer kinetics. This low RCT demonstrates the efficient transport of photogenerated charge carriers across the electrode/electrolyte interface, thereby facilitating the photoelectrochemical reaction and contributing to the enhanced hydrogen evolution performance in seawater.

      3 Conclusions

      A Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP heterostructured photocathode was successfully engineered through the integration of oxygen-deficient CuO1−X, conductive polymer layers, and Ti3C2 MXene for photoelectrochemical hydrogen generation in seawater electrolyte. Structural, optical, and electrochemical investigations revealed that the incorporation of Ti3C2 significantly enhances the photoresponse by promoting charge-carrier extraction and transport across the electrode architecture. The coexistence of oxygen-vacancy-rich CuO1−X domains, p-type PNMP and P1HP polymers, and partially oxidized Ti3C2 containing TiO2-derived n-type regions establishes favorable interfacial charge-transfer pathways and suppresses electron–hole recombination. As a result, the photocurrent density increased from −0.05 to −0.13 mA cm−2 following MXene incorporation. The optimized Ti3C2/CuO1−X–PNMP/P1HP photocathode achieved a hydrogen evolution rate of approximately 6 µmol h−1 cm−2 in seawater electrolyte. The enhanced performance is attributed to the synergistic coupling between the conductive MXene framework, defect-engineered CuO1−X semiconductor, and polymeric charge-transport network, which collectively improve light utilization and photoinduced charge separation. These findings highlight the potential of MXene-assisted oxygen-deficient semiconductor/polymer heterostructures as cost-effective and scalable platforms for sustainable hydrogen production directly from abundant seawater resources.
